2. How an article reaches the website

  1. Someone submits the news form. The form asks for a title, category (News or Event), date, their name and role, and the article. You can use the same form for your own articles.
  2. It appears in the News sheet. Open the tab called Form Responses 1. Each submission is one row, and new rows are highlighted yellow until you approve them.
  3. Read it. If it needs a fix, edit the words directly in the cell. Check there are no children’s full names or photos without a parent’s agreement.
  4. Choose a Topic. In the Topic column pick from the dropdown (Sports, Clubs, Celebrations…). The article then also appears on that Activities page and in the Gallery. Leave it empty if none fits.
  5. Add photos (optional). See “Adding photos” below.
  6. Approve it. In the Approved column choose Yes. Nothing goes on the website until it says Yes. The yellow highlight disappears.
  7. The site updates at the next rebuild. The website rebuilds itself every morning. See “Publish now” below for doing it sooner.
Allow a few minutes. After you set Approved to Yes, Google takes a few minutes to update the public copy of the sheet. A rebuild started in that window can miss your article. Wait, then rebuild.

Adding photos

  1. Open the photo folder (link in section 1) and drag your photos in. Use only photos you are allowed to publish.
  2. Right-click a photo, choose Share → Copy link.
  3. In the News sheet, click the article’s Photos cell and paste the link. For more than one photo, press Alt+Enter (Windows) or Option+Enter (Mac) inside the cell for a new line and paste the next link. Links can also be separated by a space or a comma.
  4. Optional caption: type | after the link, then the words, for example …/view | The relay team at the finish.

Photos sent through the form (if enabled). If the news form has a Photo upload question, contributors attach photos there and they appear in a Photo upload column. You do nothing extra: approving the row (Yes) makes those photos usable. They stay private until you approve. Contributors must sign in with a Google account to use that form. Only photos are published; other file types are skipped.

iPhone photos (HEIC) work as they are; no need to convert them. Up to 12 photos per article. The first photo becomes the cover image. Photos are resized and location data is removed automatically when the site rebuilds.

Publish now

In the News sheet menu bar there is a menu called Oxford site. Choose Publish approved articles now to update the website within about two minutes instead of waiting for the morning.

Current status: this button works only once the website is hosted and its rebuild link has been saved (Oxford site → Set rebuild link…). Until then, ask the person who built the site to rebuild it.

3. Writing and approving well

Category and date

  • News: something that has happened. Use the day it happened.
  • Event: something coming up. Use the day of the event. It shows under “Upcoming events” until that day has passed, then under “Past events”.

Article text

  • Plain text only. Bold, colours and links from other programs do not carry over; a web address shows as ordinary text.
  • Press Enter to start a new paragraph.
  • Keep titles short. Very long titles still display, but read better when brief.

Where articles appear

PlaceWhat it shows
Home pageThe latest 3 items. Upcoming events come first, then the newest news.
News pageThe latest 20 news items.
Events pageAll upcoming events and the 12 most recent past events.
Activities pagesThe latest articles with the matching Topic (for example Sports).

ArchivesEverything older. Nothing is ever deleted automatically.
Its own pageEvery approved article has a page that can be shared by link. Its web address is created automatically (Slug column) and stays the same even if you change the title.

Taking an article down

  1. In the News sheet, change Approved to No, or delete the row.
  2. Rebuild the website (use Oxford site → Publish approved articles now once it is connected). Do this straight away if a parent has withdrawn consent.

Copies already saved by search engines or other websites cannot be recalled by the site.

4. Admissions enquiries

  • Every enquiry and visit booking lands as a new row in the Enquiries sheet.
  • The website promises families a reply within two working days. Visit times are confirmed by phone or email.
  • The privacy page says enquiries are kept while a conversation continues and for up to twelve months afterwards, then deleted. Delete old rows accordingly.
  • Keep this sheet private: share it only with people who handle admissions.

5. Please don’t change these

The website finds each column by its heading, so a few changes will stop articles appearing.

Do notWhy
Rename a column heading in the News sheet (Title, Category, Date, Approved, Topic, Photos, Slug…) or the wording that starts a form questionThe website can no longer find the column. Fixing a typo later in a question’s wording is fine.
Delete the Approved, Topic, Photos or Slug columnsArticles stop appearing, or lose their topic, photos or permanent address.
Change the Slug of an article that is already liveIts old web address stops working.
Edit, rename or delete the Published tabIt is filled in automatically and is what the website reads.
Share the Enquiries sheet or the website’s public data linkEnquiries hold personal details of families and children.

6. If something looks wrong

What you seeWhat to check
An approved article is not on the website1. Is Approved exactly Yes? 2. Does it show in the Published tab? 3. Have you waited for a rebuild (morning, or Publish now) plus a few minutes? 4. Does the row have a date, a title and article text? A row missing any of these is skipped.
An article is live but its photo is missingCheck the photo link was pasted in the Photos cell of that row, the photo is inside the school photo folder, and the link opens when you are signed out. Then rebuild. A bad photo link never blocks the article; it is just skipped.
The Published tab shows an error or looks empty when it shouldn’tDo not edit it. Tell the person who built the site.
A date looks strange (a five-digit number)The website understands it, but tell the person who built the site so the formula can be checked.
No email alert for a new submissionCheck spam. Alerts go to the Google account that ran the setup. Another person can turn on Google’s own alert: open the form, Responses tab, three dots, “Get email notifications for new responses”.
The website did not rebuild in the morningUse Oxford site → Publish approved articles now. If it says no working link, the rebuild link needs saving again.
